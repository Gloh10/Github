Session-anchored VWAP on 5-min NQ bars, vs. buy-and-hold NQ. No S&P 500 line here — this window is short enough (and NQ trades near-24h vs. SPX's ~6.5h session) that an intraday SPX comparison wouldn't be meaningful; see the daily chart above for the actual vs.-S&P-500 comparison.

Read this before drawing conclusions:
  • Setup 1 (Sub-VWAP Trap) is a mechanical approximation. The original strategy's entry trigger is read from live level-2 order flow and time-and-sales — data historical OHLCV bars don't contain. This substitutes "close above VWAP after 1-2 failed reclaim attempts." At the literal 10% squeeze threshold from the source video (small-cap context), it found zero qualifying setups on NQ in both windows — a real finding: that precondition essentially never occurs on a large liquid index future. Even relaxed thresholds found at most 1 trade. Treat Setup 1's results as "not enough data to evaluate on this instrument," not as a real edge or non-edge.
  • Setups 2 and 3 are the genuinely mechanical ones (VWAP + standard-deviation bands + candlestick-pattern confirmation) and are fairly tested here.
  • No slippage, commissions, or spread modeled. Real execution costs would reduce every strategy's results, likely more for the intraday ones (more trades) than the daily ones.
  • Same-bar stop/target conflicts resolve to the stop (conservative assumption) — if a single bar's range touches both levels, the trade is scored a loss.
  • Fixed 1% account risk per trade assumed for every strategy's equity curve, purely to make the curves comparable — not a recommendation.
  • This is a hypothetical backtest for research purposes, not investment advice, and past performance (real or simulated) doesn't predict future results.
