Six setups extracted from trading-education video transcripts (VWAP mean reversion & trend continuation, a sub-VWAP trap, an ICT-style Standard Deviation + OTE model, and a swing-failure-pattern + fair-value-gap model), tested mechanically against historical NQ (Nasdaq-100 futures) bars, plus 12 hand-built combinations of those setups. Compared to buy-and-hold S&P 500 on the statistically meaningful daily window. Read the multiple-comparisons warning below before treating any single result as a real edge.

Read this before drawing conclusions — especially about the combinations:
  • The combinations were built by testing multiple ideas against the same historical sample and reporting the best one — that's a textbook overfitting setup. The more variants tried, the more likely one looks good by chance alone, with no real edge. The full ranked table above exists specifically so the "winner" isn't shown in isolation. The top result (daily regime-adaptive: mean-reversion + trend-continuation merged, +16.5R/32 trades) is the most defensible of the 12 combos, since it's just the union of two components already tested independently rather than a newly-tuned confluence filter — but "most defensible of 12" is not the same as "validated." The only real test would be running it forward on data it hasn't seen yet.
  • Setup 1 (Sub-VWAP Trap) is a mechanical approximation whose entry trigger is normally read from live level-2 order flow, which historical bars don't contain. It found ~0 qualifying setups on NQ — a real finding (that precondition essentially never occurs on a large liquid index future), not a bug.
  • Setup 5 (Standard Deviation + OTE) and Setup 6 (SFP + FVG) both required real interpretive choices to make codeable at all — fractal pivots standing in for by-eye swing selection, order blocks not modeled, "key opens" reduced to the daily open. See their individual write-ups for the specific approximations.
  • No slippage, commissions, or spread modeled. Real execution costs would reduce every result, more for the intraday/higher-trade-count ones than the daily ones.
  • Same-bar stop/target conflicts resolve to the stop (conservative assumption).
  • Fixed 1% account risk per trade assumed for every equity curve, purely to make curves comparable — not a recommendation.
  • Hypothetical backtest for research purposes, not investment advice. Past performance (real or simulated) doesn't predict future results.
