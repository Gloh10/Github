## 2. High-Density Energy Sources Report

High-density energy — nuclear (incl. SMRs), nuclear-hydrogen hybrids, and
next-gen batteries — continues to be driven by AI/data-center power demand
and geopolitical supply shifts.

### Nuclear / SMRs
- SMRs are moving from demonstration to commercial deployment: over **$1.3B**
  in equity investment in 2025, and cumulative U.S. federal investment in new
  nuclear exceeding **$10B** since 2020.
- The NRC issued a construction permit for TerraPower's Natrium plant
  (Kemmerer, WY) in March 2026 — the first for a commercial non-light-water
  reactor in 40+ years; construction began the following month.
- Meta signed with TerraPower for up to 8 Natrium units by 2035 (first units
  targeted 2032). The US and Japan announced a joint $40B SMR initiative
  (GE Vernova + Hitachi) for plants in Tennessee and Alabama.
- Nuclear supplies ~15% of global data-center electricity in 2026; conditional
  offtake agreements between data centers and SMR projects nearly doubled
  from 25GW (end of 2024) to 45GW today.
- DOE awarded $400M each to TVA and Holtec as part of a $900M 2025
  solicitation to accelerate SMR deployment.

### Nuclear-Hydrogen Integration
- NewHydrogen is pairing high-temperature SMR heat with its ThermoLoop
  process for industrial-scale clean hydrogen via thermochemical water
  splitting — targeting a share of the ~$5T annual global energy market.
- Multiple DOE-backed nuclear-hydrogen demonstrations are underway
  (including Xcel Energy SOEC projects).

### High-Density Batteries
- China's October 2025 export restrictions on batteries above 300 Wh/kg are
  reshaping Western supply chains, accelerating domestic production buildout
  across the US, Europe, Korea, and Japan.
- Silicon-carbon composite anodes have moved from lab to mass commercial
  deployment — first in premium consumer electronics, now expanding into
  automotive — and are the dominant near-term path to higher energy density.
- Semi-solid-state designs are emerging as the practical bridge to full
  solid-state batteries: meaningful density gains plus safety improvements
  over liquid electrolyte Li-ion.
- New demand driver: military/defense drones (accelerated by combat use in
  Ukraine and Pentagon procurement), adding a fast-growing segment distinct
  from EV/grid storage.
- Li-ion still leads 2026 market share (~32.9%) on cost and density; Asia
  Pacific leads regional share (~42%).

### Read-through for the portfolio
- SMR/nuclear supply chain (reactor OEMs, uranium/fuel, grid interconnect)
  and hyperscaler-linked offtake plays are the most direct expressions.
- Battery names exposed to silicon-carbon anode tech or semi-solid-state IP
  may benefit from the density/safety inflection; names dependent on
  Chinese high-density cell imports carry supply-chain risk from the export
  restrictions.
